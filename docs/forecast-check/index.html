Would the 64 kt trigger have fired on the forecasts?

The proposed trigger — 5,000 people exposed to 64 kt winds in Shefa, Sanma and Tafea — was designed on observed storm tracks. This page re-runs it against the forecasts that were actually on the table at the time, to see which storms would have triggered an activation in real time.

What the numbers are, and what they are not

Forecast exposure is computed from JTWC official forecasts in the VMGD historical archive: the 34/50/64 kt quadrant wind radii from each forecast cycle are swept along the forecast track into a wind swath, and WorldPop population inside the three AOI provinces is summed. This uses the same method and the same population raster as the observed exposure numbers, so the two are directly comparable.

VMGD's own forecast tracks carry no wind radii — their Forecast Track Maps give position, intensity category and a position-accuracy figure only, with the wind boundaries drawn on the map image. They are shown here as tracks for context, but they cannot produce an exposure number.

Each storm's peak value is the highest exposure across all of its forecast cycles — i.e. would the trigger have fired at any point during the event. Invest-numbered decks (90–99) are excluded because they duplicate the named storm.

Exposure counts only Shefa, Sanma and Tafea (39 of Vanuatu's 66 adm2 units), total population —, which is the ceiling for every number on this page. Two artifacts in the original per-adm2 exposure table on blob are corrected here (and in adm2_usaradii_exp_dedup.parquet): per-adm2 all_touched clips counted shared boundary pixels more than once (+23% on AOI totals), and swaths crossing the 180° meridian were torn by the coordinate conversion into shapes that spuriously covered Vanuatu — Fiji storms like Tomas, Yasa, Evan and Keni showed large exposure they never had. All buffers here are handled in a longitude-wrapped frame, so figures read substantially lower than the uncorrected ones.
